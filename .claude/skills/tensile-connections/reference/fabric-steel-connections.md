# Fabric-to-steel (rigid boundary) connections

## 1. Keder (bolt rope) in an aluminium track
* Keder = PVC or EPDM rod welded into a small edge pocket. It is trapped behind clamp plates bolted to the steel, or slid into a
  luff-track extrusion [V].
* Sizes 6–12 mm common (7, 8 mm; tent systems 10/12/16 mm) [V]. Slot opening ≈ **60–70 % of keder Ø** [V].
* Load path: keder bearing on the plate edge plus friction membrane-to-plate [V]. Choose Ø so it cannot squeeze through the gap.
* Alloys 6063-T5/T6/T66, 6005/6061-T6; anodised or powder coated [V]. Example rail: 6005-T6, 51.5 × 25 mm, 1.204 kg/m, 6 m [V].
* Double keder for adjustable tracks and field joints; PTFE uses fixed or adjustable keder track [V].

## 2. Clamp bars
* Aluminium 6082-T6 flat bar 8–12 × 40–60 mm, segments about 300–600 mm (short on curved edges) [U]; bolts M10–M12
  (M16 heavy PTFE) in A4 stainless [U]; spacing 100–200 mm, **≤ ~200 mm** [V, TensiNet].
* Stagger segment butt joints and **chamfer or radius the segment ends** to avoid tears at joints [V, FA].
* Align field seams with cable positions so a cable rides over a standard double clamp joint [V].
* Design: bolt tension from the membrane force n (kN/m) × spacing, with prying; plate bending between bolts; friction and bearing on the keder.

## 3. Adjustable rigid edge and lacing
* Keder track or clamp bar on threaded studs or slotted brackets (e.g. every 300–500 mm [U]) so the edge can be drawn out.
* Lace-on (grommets plus cord to a pipe): small or temporary PVC work only; low stiffness; re-tension.

## 4. Arches, ridges and valleys on steel
* Membrane over an arch on a sleeve or pad with a PVC/PE protective strip, or clamped both sides with keder rails on fins [U].
  Continuous clamping makes the arch a rigid boundary (the membrane cannot slide), which changes the stress pattern.

## 5. Mast head, high point, cone ring ("tulip")
* Cones: membrane tensioned between a top **bale ring** and the perimeter supports [V].
* Roped edge between clamp-plate pairs connected to **jaw-end turnbuckles on ear plates of the mast**. Clamp bars are jointed to
  articulate around radial cables. Cheap for irregular peaks but needs careful detailing against tears [V, FA Part 2].
* The peak often houses the tensioning devices and must anchor radial cables below the fabric (to the ring or the mast head) [V].
* Fit a rain cap ("hat") over the ring opening.

## 6. Low points and base connections
* Low-point membrane plate or ring clamped to the fabric, then fork/turnbuckle to an anchor plate in the foundation.
* Provide drainage at low points; keep the fabric clear of the ground (vandalism, abrasion).

## 7. Materials and bimetallic isolation [V]
* Aluminium is anodic to stainless. Corrosion rate rises with the cathode/anode area ratio: small stainless bolts in large aluminium parts is the
  favourable case. Isolate with non-conductive washers **and hole sleeves** (continuous), EPDM/PVC tape under clamp bars on galvanised
  steel, sealant and paint. Radius all aluminium arrises touching the membrane.
* Fixings: A2/A4 (EN ISO 3506) property class 70/80; A4 near the coast or pools.
